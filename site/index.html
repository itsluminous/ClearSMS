See it in action

Real Clear SMS screens from a phone with a handful of bank, delivery and OTP messages on it.

A smart inbox with nothing hidden

Every message is sorted by a rule you can open and read. There is no model to second-guess: a rule is a sender pattern, a body pattern or both, a category, and a name. This is one of the 508 that ship with the app.

Six categories, your order

Important, Promotional, Personal, OTP, Unknown and Spam. Choose which category pills show on each tab and drag them into the order you want. Tap the title bar to jump back to the top.

Rules you can read, write and share

508 community rules and a 715k sender directory come bundled. Add your own with one tap from any message ("Always sort as"), or write a regex in the full editor, which explains any rejection. Search, disable, edit, duplicate, export and share them from Settings.

Search that finds the message

Full-text search with category and time filters matches contact and sender names as well as message text. Tapping a result opens the conversation at that message and highlights it, however old it is.

Scam awareness

Likely scam and fraud messages are flagged with simple heuristics, and a tap on a link or number in a flagged message warns you first. Block senders or keywords and they go straight to the bin, silently.

A complete messaging app

SMS and MMS send and receive as your default messaging app, with drafts, scheduling, an optional send delay with Cancel, pinned and muted conversations, a 30-day recycle bin, undo for delete and archive, and launcher shortcuts for recent conversations.

Dual-SIM, done properly

Per-recipient SIM memory, SIM tags on messages, and a compose bar that shows which slot will send. Images are compressed on the phone to the sending SIM's carrier limit before an MMS goes anywhere.

Your bank SMS, turned into a dashboard

Banks already text you every debit, credit and due date. Clear SMS reads those messages on the phone and builds the view your banking apps never agree on.

Accounts, cards and wallets

Transactions are extracted into accounts, credit cards and wallets, with monthly debit and credit charts drawn by the app itself. Duplicate UPI alerts from two banks collapse into one; retirement contributions count as credits.

Balances behind your screen lock

Balance tracking with an optional biometric lock: fingerprint, face, PIN, pattern or password, whatever your phone already uses. Amounts stay in the message's own currency and are never summed across currencies.

Bills and due dates in one place

Upcoming bills, autopay, insurance and credit-card statements in one Alerts tab, including undated bills. Older alerts move to a restorable archive instead of disappearing.

Journeys and deliveries

Train and flight bookings, including compact Indian Railways PNR messages, and parcel deliveries with courier and tracking id, each as a card you can dismiss.

OTPs you never retype

Big, copyable OTP notifications with Copy, Mark read and Delete, optional auto-copy, an always-visible Copy button on OTP messages, and auto-delete after 24 hours, 3 days, 7 days or never.

Notifications that say what happened

Transaction notifications show the amount in red, green or blue with the bank's logo. Notifications clear when you read the message in the app, and messages missed during signal loss get their own catch-up notification.

Private by design, stated precisely

The whole argument for Clear SMS is that your messages stay on your phone. These are the exact claims, including the two nuances the README spells out.

No INTERNET permission

The app requests no INTERNET permission and makes no network calls of its own: no servers, no telemetry, no analytics.

The one exception is inherent to MMS. Retrieving or sending a picture message is a transaction the Android system's MMS service performs with your carrier's MMSC over the carrier network. That is how the MMS protocol works; it never leaves the carrier network and involves no third party.

Permissions, named honestly

The manifest asks for what a default SMS app needs, plus one opt-in: the phone-call permission behind Settings → Messages → Direct call.

Android offers no way to declare a permission conditionally, so "make and manage phone calls" appears in the app's permission list for everyone. It is requested only at the moment you switch Direct call on, and while the toggle is off (the default) every Call button opens the dialer prefilled, exactly as it would without the permission.

No proprietary dependencies

No Firebase, no Play Services: pure AOSP-compatible code, released under the Apache License 2.0. Every categorisation rule is human-readable JSON you can inspect, edit, export and contribute back.

Your data stays on your device

Backups of messages and settings are plain files written to a folder you choose, on a schedule if you like. They are not encrypted, so keep them somewhere you trust. The balance lock and Direct call settings never ride in a backup, by design.

When you report a bug, the diagnostic log records no message text, contacts, numbers, OTPs or account numbers. You preview the exact text before sharing it and choose whether sender ids are masked.

Three steps, nothing to sign up for

There are no accounts and no servers. The app only needs to be the one Android hands your messages to.

Install it

From F-Droid, GitHub Releases or Obtainium. Both the F-Droid build and the GitHub APK carry the same signature, so you can install from one and update from the other.

Make it your default SMS app

Android asks on first launch. Clear SMS then sends and receives your messages, and the messages already on the phone, picture messages included, are imported from the system store.

Open Inbox, Finance and Alerts

Categories, accounts and due dates come from the messages themselves. When an update ships new rules, the inbox re-sorts itself, with a progress banner while it works.
